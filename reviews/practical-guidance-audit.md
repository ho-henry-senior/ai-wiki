# Practical guidance audit: value and low-friction adoption

**Review date:** 2026-11-15  
**Scope:** Practical guidance linked from the Alignment, Economy, Traceability, and Security overviews, plus closely related pages that inform those routes.

## Scope and method

I followed the homepage and four outcome overviews, then read the linked practical pages and directly overlapping material. The assessment covers the five requested criteria: agent-specific value, actionability, adoption cost, likely payoff, and distinctiveness. “High,” “medium,” and “low” are editorial judgments, not measured outcomes. Adoption cost is rated from low to high; the other criteria are rated from low to high. Each recommendation is primary: **KEEP**, **SHARPEN**, **CONSOLIDATE**, or **REMOVE**.

The table cites page sections as evidence. Recommendations and scores are editorial judgments about that evidence, not quotations from the pages. I did not independently verify external product behavior, linked research, or changing claims. The case study’s own limits on measured outcomes are retained below.

This is a scoped audit of the practical guidance routes, not a whole-wiki content or freshness review. The glossary, unrelated pages, raw source material, and slides were not audited. Directly supporting concepts, the relevant case study, and the explanatory model/harness article are treated separately from how-to guidance.

## Specific examples of low-value or difficult advice

- **Generic unless made concrete:** “Make responsibilities visible” in [Traceability’s core advice](../pages/traceability.md#core-advice) does not say how a beginner should record who reviews or approves a small change. Clear ownership is useful, but the agent-specific benefit and simplest action need to be explicit.
- **Repeated task-interface advice:** The general case for deterministic commands and explicit contracts appears in [delegating repository tasks](../pages/agent-tool-delegation.md), the [Justfile pattern](../pages/justfile-as-agent-facing-task-interface.md), and [tool choices by purpose](../pages/tools-by-purpose.md), with the reusable contract in the [task-interface concept](../concepts/task-interface-contracts.md). The overlap obscures the actual choice: try existing scripts first; add a wrapper only when it solves a real discovery or consistency problem.
- **Repeated security checks:** Access, credentials, network, integrations, and artifact review recur in the [security overview](../pages/security.md), the [sensitive-data guide](../pages/sensitive-data-in-agent-workflows.md), the [data-flow checklist](../pages/agent-data-flow-checklist.md), and the [local](../pages/local-agent-security.md)/[cloud](../pages/cloud-agent-security.md) guides. The repetition can reassure readers, but it also makes the beginner route feel like four checklists. Preserve one narrative hub, one reusable data-flow checklist, and the distinct local/cloud controls.
- **Costly without prioritization:** [Repository context](../pages/repository-context-for-agents.md) discusses ADRs, cross-repository contracts, and diagrams. These can prevent important errors, but a beginner needs help deciding which missing fact is worth documenting before taking on that maintenance.
- **A shortlist without demonstrated payoff:** The [useful-skills list](../pages/useful-skills.md) transparently labels entries untried and built-in equivalents unassessed. That is honest research, but it falls short of a practical low-friction recommendation until setup, compatibility, and benefit are established.
- **Common engineering advice with a meaningful agent angle:** Focused tests, task acceptance criteria, least privilege, human review, and explicit task contracts are not novel practices. Their case here is that agents can act quickly, misunderstand underspecified tasks, and produce tests that share a mistaken assumption with generated code. Keep them where the pages explain that difference and make a first action easy.

## Five highest-priority editorial changes

1. **Make Traceability distinct and demonstrable.** Keep `define-and-verify-tasks.md` as the shared task workflow. In `traceability.md`, avoid repeating its outcome/criteria/checks sequence; use one small end-to-end example linking request, change, checks, review, and decision, and make “responsibilities visible” enactable without requiring a multi-agent process.
2. **Consolidate task-interface guidance.** Keep `agent-tool-delegation.md` as the practical general guide and `task-interface-contracts.md` as the reusable contract. Reduce `justfile-as-agent-facing-task-interface.md` to the Just-specific example and when-not-to-add advice; consolidate or slim `tools-by-purpose.md` while preserving its useful observed examples.
3. **Give security one clear narrative route.** Keep the local, cloud, sandbox, and prompt-injection pages because they address different execution risks. Make `sensitive-data-in-agent-workflows.md` explain the common model, with the data-flow checklist as the operational review; remove repeated baseline checklists without weakening safeguards. Align the security overview’s stated status and surfaced routes with its actual contents in a later, separately approved navigation change.
4. **Separate the minimum context step from optional repository architecture.** Keep `agents-md-as-shared-entrypoint.md` as the concrete entrypoint technique and link to it from `repository-context-for-agents.md` instead of repeating the same starting advice. Make it unmistakable that ADRs, cross-repository maps, and the full repository layout are selective follow-ups, not a required setup project.
5. **Raise the evidence bar for skills and agent-role advice.** Present skills as optional when a recurring task and harness gap justify them; only call a skill practically useful when compatibility, setup effort, and evidence support that claim. Keep the role/profile distinction, but condense the large illustrative RACI workflow or make its optional, higher-cost nature unmistakable.

## Potential gaps in beginner guidance

- **A compact traceability example:** The task guide has a useful fictional issue/PR example, but the overview identifies a lack of detail for following intent across artifacts and decisions. One proportionate example could show when a link is sufficient and what a reviewer should be able to verify.
- **A way to choose context work:** The repository-context page raises useful questions but does not yet help a beginner prioritize which missing project fact to record first. Avoid prescribing ADRs or diagrams before a real recurring ambiguity justifies them.
- **Evidence-backed skill trials:** The skills material explains selection criteria, but the current shortlist has no tried candidates and no assessment of built-in equivalents. A small, low-setup example with an explicit result may be more useful than a broad list; do not add one without evidence.
- **A genuinely low-effort security start:** Security pages appropriately ask readers to inspect real permissions and destinations, which vary by harness and environment. A portable checklist can identify the questions, but cannot supply universal settings or prove a setup secure. Beginner guidance should distinguish a first risk check from the stronger controls needed for sensitive or unattended work rather than promise a one-size-fits-all safe default.

## Tensions with the design goals

- The goals prioritize clear benefit and modest effort, while security may require substantial verification or enforced isolation. The design goals explicitly say essential controls are not optional because they take more work; editorial simplification should not weaken that safeguard.
- Small canonical concepts and linked articles support reuse and maintenance, but the resulting number of pages, concepts, and cross-links can burden beginners. Curated “try this first” actions should remain short and optional detail should not read as a prerequisite.
- Portable, durable guidance can be less immediately actionable than product-specific setup steps. The pages generally label Copilot- or harness-specific behavior and its limits; preserving that qualification is preferable to making a broader but misleading promise.
- Some familiar engineering practices are necessary foundations, not distinctive new advice. Retain them where the pages make clear what agents change about the risk or payoff; avoid presenting generic slogans as sufficient agent guidance.
- The case study provides useful observed experience but no measured time/cost baseline. Its candid limits support the design goal of preserving evidence, while also showing that some likely benefits remain plausible rather than quantified.

## Boundaries and handoff

This report makes recommendations only; no existing guidance page, navigation, design goal, or automation was changed. The recommendations identify editorial work for human review and separate follow-up issues. The sitemap status/path discrepancy above is reported, not repaired here.
